Ongoing & Past Research

My research focuses on full-stack humanoid robotics—spanning mechanical design, hardware integration, real-time control, and learning-based optimization.

DRACO 4: Next-Generation Humanoid Platform

Human Centered Robotics Lab | Graduate Research Assistant | Jan 2025 – Present

I am leading the end-to-end development of DRACO 4, a next-generation full-sized humanoid designed to address the limitations of its predecessor while advancing the state of humanoid loco-manipulation research.

The Challenge

DRACO 3 was a successful humanoid platform with innovative rolling contact joints in the lower body. However, its design introduced significant challenges: the rolling contacts complicated control implementation, and inaccessible electronics placement made maintenance and upgrades nearly impossible. These issues motivated our lab to develop DRACO 4 with a fundamentally different design philosophy.

Design Philosophy: Maintainable, Modular, Documented, Robust

DRACO 4 is being developed as a maintainable and upgradable research platform with comprehensive documentation. Every design decision prioritizes accessibility for future researchers and ease of hardware servicing. The platform features a novel, low-inertia upper body limb design that enables both high-performance manipulation and safe human-robot interaction.

My Contributions

  • Mechanical Design & Fabrication: Designed and manufactured the entire lower body using CAD (SolidWorks), including structural components, joint assemblies, and integration points for electronics and actuators.
  • Hardware Integration: Fabricated and assembled mechanical components, integrated electronic hardware systems including motor drivers, sensors, and computing infrastructure.
  • Real-Time Communication: Developed a ROS2/EtherCAT communication interface using the SOEM (Simple Open EtherCAT Master) library to establish deterministic, real-time communication between the control computer and robot hardware with sub-millisecond latency.
  • Learning-Based Morphology Optimization: Conducting research on learning-based morphology optimization using PyTorch in Isaac Lab and Mujoco simulation environments. Focusing on exploring oblique lower body axis configurations to optimize for both dynamic performance and energy efficiency.
  • Simulation Development: Built high-fidelity physics simulations in Isaac Lab for rapid prototyping of morphological variations and control strategies before physical implementation.

Technical Stack

Software: ROS2, EtherCAT/SOEM, PyTorch, Isaac Lab, Mujoco, Python, C++
Hardware: SolidWorks CAD, 3D printing, CNC machining, motor driver integration, sensor calibration

Impact

DRACO 4 will serve as the primary research platform for humanoid loco-manipulation at the HCR lab, enabling future research in whole-body control, learning-based locomotion, and human-robot collaboration.

BreathIQ: Smart Respiratory Monitoring

Hutter Research Group | Undergraduate Research Assistant | Aug 2023 – May 2025

Researched air flow sensor technologies and analyzed 50+ patient datasets using Principal Component Analysis (PCA). Developed a Python-based UI for real-time data collection and visualization.

Impact: Co-authored presentation poster for the 2024 Defense TechConnect Innovation Summit and Expo.

Technologies: SolidWorks, Python, PCA, sensor integration, medical device prototyping

NeuFinger: Cable-Driven Robotic Finger

Rehabilitation and Neuromuscular Robotics Lab | Undergraduate Research Assistant | June 2024 – Aug 2024

Fabricated and assembled a pair of cable-pulley robotic fingers (4 DOF total: 2×2 DOF), improving structural integrity and minimizing joint play. Developed 2 prototype tensioner mechanisms to maintain cable system reliability during dynamic experiments.

Assisted in sensorless force estimation research by configuring and calibrating GL80 KV30 actuators for precise torque control.

Technologies: Cable-driven mechanisms, actuator calibration, mechanical fabrication, force estimation
